# 07 — India vs Global Market Test

Date: 13 October 2026

## Executive recommendation

**Do not launch two markets at once.** Keep the landing page and product broadly understandable in English, but run a small India-first discovery test while recruiting a few global comparison interviews through existing networks if they are easy to reach. Do not build region-specific features, pricing, or paid acquisition until the first conversations show a real difference.

Why this is the default:
- The founders can likely reach local families and get faster feedback in India; this is an execution hypothesis to verify, not a proven channel advantage.
- India-specific research and competitor examples exist, but they do not validate Renomedy demand.
- U.S. caregiver statistics show a large, documented audience, but size alone does not establish willingness to adopt or pay for Renomedy.

## Evidence snapshot

### India

A 2025 cross-sectional study published in *Psychogeriatrics* surveyed 1,000 caregivers and 1,265 older adults in an urban locality in India. It found variation in caregiver burden associated with older adults' dependency level. The sample is not a national market-size estimate and does not specifically test medication-management apps.  
Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/psyg.13245

A 2025 paper in the *Journal of Social and Economic Development* discusses the rise of app-based later-life care in India and reflects on the sector's development. This supports examining the existing category, not assuming that demand for Renomedy is established.  
Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40847-025-00472-7

Public competitor pages include India-based elder-care and family coordination offerings. Treat their claims and pricing as company-authored positioning, not independently verified performance.

### Global comparison: United States as the first reference market

AARP and the National Alliance for Caregiving reported in 2025 that 63 million U.S. adults provided care for an adult or child with a complex medical condition or disability in the prior year; roughly one in four adults was a caregiver. A 2026 companion report says more than 51 million Americans cared for someone aged 50 or older in 2025. These figures establish that family caregiving is a substantial issue in the U.S.; they do not measure the market for Renomedy or prove willingness to pay.  
Sources:
- https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/ltss/family-caregiving/caregiving-in-the-us-2025/
- https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/ltss/family-caregiving/family-caregiving-in-us-2025/

FTC guidance states that health-related product claims must be truthful and adequately substantiated, and that health apps should review privacy/security practices and ensure their consumer-facing statements match actual data practices. This is relevant to any U.S. market test; local legal advice may be needed before commercial launch.  
Sources:
- https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance
- https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security/health-privacy

## Working comparison

| Dimension | India-first test | U.S. / global comparison test |
|---|---|---|
| Problem evidence | Caregiver research exists, but Renomedy-specific demand remains unproven | Strong public caregiver prevalence data; Renomedy-specific demand remains unproven |
| Best initial access hypothesis | Personal/community introductions, relevant caregiver groups, local elder-care organizations | Existing personal/professional networks, caregiver communities, relevant online groups where research is permitted |
| Message to test | Keeping a parent's medicine information organized and making family handoffs clearer | Same core job; test wording that fits the target user's context |
| Primary risk | Assuming one urban sample represents all of India; underestimating trust, language, and affordability differences | Assuming broad caregiver prevalence translates into app adoption; underestimating competition, trust, and privacy expectations |
| Product requirements | Verify access, consent, language needs, privacy, and supported workflows | Same, plus careful review of applicable U.S. privacy and consumer-protection obligations before launch |
| Cost/time | Potentially easier founder access, to verify | Potentially harder access if founders lack a trusted network, to verify |
| Decision metric | Qualified conversations, guided trials, activation, repeat use | Same metrics, measured separately |

## Four-week market experiment

### Week 1 — Interview access check
- Recruit 2–3 India-based adult children through permission-based introductions.
- Recruit 1–2 U.S./global adult children only if an existing network makes this low-effort.
- Ask about their last real coordination task, current workaround, what went wrong, and how often it occurs. Do not ask for medical details or patient identifiers.
- Do not pitch until the workflow is understood.

### Week 2 — Message test
Test one clear promise: “Keep a parent's medicine information organized and make family handoffs easier.” Adapt wording only when interviews show a meaningful language/context difference. Track which source each conversation came from.

### Week 3 — Guided trial
Invite qualified participants to use one existing Renomedy workflow. Observe task completion and confusion. Use fictional data unless the product has been reviewed for safe handling of real health data and participants have given informed consent.

### Week 4 — Choose the next market focus
Compare by market:
- qualified conversations per hour of founder effort
- share of interviewees with the target problem
- guided trial starts
- first-value completion
- whether users return or request to share with family
- trust/privacy objections and feature gaps

With small samples, use the results directionally, not as statistical proof. If one market has too few conversations, report it as insufficient evidence rather than a loss.

## Decision rule

Continue testing a market only if it produces credible problem evidence and a practical route to guided trials within the 2–3 combined hours/week budget. Do not pick a market based solely on population, app-download counts, competitor claims, or a few compliments.

## What remains unknown

- Actual reachable audience for Renomedy in either market
- Willingness to trust the product with medicine-related information
- Frequency of the specific task Renomedy solves
- Whether the current onboarding works for new users
- Whether users return and whether any segment will pay
- Legal/privacy requirements for the exact data flows and launch model

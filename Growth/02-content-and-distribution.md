# 02 — Content and Distribution Playbook

## Purpose

Build trust and attract a small number of relevant families with a sustainable workload. Content is a learning and distribution asset, not a requirement to post every day.

## Audience hypothesis

Adult children who actively help a parent organize medicines and coordinate family responsibilities. Do not assume every caregiver has the same needs; use conversations to refine the segment.

## Content pillars

### A. Family organization
Examples:
- “A simple way to keep a parent's current medicine list organized for appointments”
- “How to divide healthcare errands across siblings”
- “A weekly family care update template”

### B. Questions to ask professionals
Examples:
- “A checklist for preparing questions before a medical appointment”
- “How to record follow-up instructions in the words your clinician used”

Make clear these are organizational aids, not diagnosis or treatment advice.

### C. Product transparency
Examples:
- Short screen recording showing how a task is organized
- A “what Renomedy can and cannot do today” post
- A build-in-public note explaining a user-requested improvement without identifying the user

### D. Caregiver workflow learning
Examples:
- An anonymized lesson from interviews, shared only with consent and without identifiable details
- A poll about how families currently share appointment updates
- A summary of common organizational approaches, without claiming prevalence from a tiny sample

## Weekly content workflow (45 minutes)

1. Choose one question heard from a real target user (5 min).
2. Draft a 150–300 word practical answer (15 min).
3. Turn it into one checklist or visual (15 min).
4. Publish once on the single channel where target users are reachable; add a clear, low-pressure CTA (5 min).
5. Log the URL, source tag, and outcome (5 min).

Repurpose the same asset; do not create separate content for every platform. Start with one channel selected from actual access to the target audience, not popularity alone.

## Suggested first four assets

1. **Care task handoff checklist:** task, owner, due date, status, question for clinician.
2. **Appointment preparation sheet:** current medicine list (verified against source), questions, tests/follow-ups mentioned by the care team.
3. **Family update template:** what changed, what is due, who owns next steps; avoid posting patient details publicly.
4. **Renomedy product walkthrough:** demonstrate one existing feature with fictional data and disclose limitations.

## CTA ladder

- Early content: “Tell us how your family handles this” / invite a short research conversation.
- After usability is stable: “Request a guided trial.”
- After repeated value is observed: “Join the next pilot.”
- Do not ask for payment before defining what is included and what ongoing value is delivered.

## Distribution rules

- Share only in communities where promotion is allowed; contribute useful information first and disclose affiliation.
- Never pretend to be a customer, clinician, or neutral reviewer.
- Do not use patient stories, photographs, or testimonials without explicit informed permission.
- Do not promise better adherence, fewer errors, or better health outcomes without evidence and appropriate review.
- Do not use fear, guilt, or urgency to pressure caregivers.
- Use subtitles and simple language; test regional-language content only when a real audience need is identified.

## Metrics

Track per asset:
- target audience and channel
- qualified conversations or demo requests
- visits tagged to the asset
- trials started and first-value completion
- time spent producing/distributing it

Likes and views are secondary. Keep an asset if it produces relevant conversations or assists activation; revise or stop it after a fair test if it produces attention but no qualified interest.

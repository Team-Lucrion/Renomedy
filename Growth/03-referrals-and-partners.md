# 03 — Referral and Partner Playbook

## Goal

Explore trusted routes to families without depending on constant social posting. Treat partners as a hypothesis to validate, not a shortcut to guaranteed customers.

## Potential partner types

Test one category at a time:
1. Caregiver support groups or community organizers
2. Elder-care coordinators and non-clinical care services
3. Clinics or healthcare professionals who are interested in family organization
4. Community organizations serving adult children who support older relatives

Pharmacies may be a later experiment only after understanding the relevant rules and whether the proposed activity is appropriate. Do not ask pharmacies or clinicians to promote prescription medicines or make clinical claims.

## Partner discovery script

> Hello, I'm part of the team building Renomedy, a family organization tool for keeping a parent's medicine information and care tasks coordinated. We're researching how families handle this today. Could I ask you a few questions about the workflow? We're not asking for patient information or an endorsement.

Ask:
- What coordination tasks do families commonly ask for help with?
- What tools do they use today?
- At what point does information-sharing become difficult?
- Who would need to approve a tool like this?
- What would make it useful or inappropriate in your setting?
- Would a neutral product walkthrough be useful after this conversation?

Do not request names, contact details, or medical information about the partner's clients or patients.

## Pilot offer

Only after product access and privacy controls are ready:
- A short, no-pressure walkthrough using fictional data
- A small opt-in pilot with clearly defined scope
- A feedback conversation after use
- No claim that the partner endorses Renomedy unless there is explicit written permission

Never pay for referrals or exchange patient information without appropriate legal, ethical, and consent review. If founders are minors, involve a trusted adult for formal agreements, payments, and professional outreach.

## Referral loop for actual users

Once a user reports a useful experience, ask:
> If you know another family member who helps coordinate a parent's care, feel free to share our pilot page with them. Please don't share anyone's health information.

No rewards are necessary for the first test. Measure whether users voluntarily share and whether referred prospects match the target segment.

## Tracking

For each partner conversation, record:
- partner category (not private client details)
- date and contact role
- workflow problem described
- interest level and next step
- whether a demo/pilot happened
- objections and time required

## Stop rules

- After five relevant conversations in a category, if no one identifies a meaningful workflow problem or next step, pause that category.
- If the partner is interested but product safety/privacy is not ready, do not run a live-patient pilot.
- Do not treat a polite introduction as distribution. Count a partner channel as promising only when it creates qualified conversations or opt-in trials.

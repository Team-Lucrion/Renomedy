Swasthi — Behavioral + Workflow Stress Testing Report

India-first chronic-care medication continuity stress test. Built to break unsafe states before beta users encounter them. Generated 2026-05-22 12:44 UTC.

Patient safety firstOCR is draft onlyVerification before activationTrust preservation

Executive read: brutal truth

Biggest product risk

Swasthi will fail dangerously if it behaves like a confident automation system. It must behave like a careful continuity assistant that preserves uncertainty until a human verifies.

Biggest retention risk

Reminder fatigue appears between Day 20–45. If the app only nags, caregivers feel burden transfer, not relief.

Biggest trust unlock

Transparent update flows: old vs new medicine, stopped items, timestamps, source prescription image, and “unmarked” instead of “missed.”

Top 25 most dangerous failure modes

  1. Duplicate activation after strength change: old and new strengths remain active together.
  2. Combo medicine overlap missed: single molecule + fixed-dose combo both scheduled.
  3. OCR converts BD to TDS or OD to BD and creates wrong frequency.
  4. Decimal dosage ambiguity: 0.5 interpreted as 5 or half-tablet not operationally possible.
  5. Hospital discharge changes added without explicit stop of old outpatient regimen.
  6. GP prescription overwrites specialist plan without conflict warning.
  7. Stale prescription continues generating refill/reminder confidence after doctor follow-up is overdue.
  8. Brand/generic duplicate not merged, causing duplicate reminders or duplicate intake.
  9. Formulation ignored: SR/ER/DSR/plain treated as same medicine.
  10. Alternate-day thyroid schedules flattened into daily reminders.
  11. Refill stock assumed rather than verified, producing false reassurance.
  12. Caregiver sees “missed” when dose is merely unmarked, causing panic and trust loss.
  13. Patient marks all medicines done without reading individual items.
  14. Notification fatigue causes users to dismiss reminders reflexively by Day 20–45.
  15. Unsupported medicine types silently allowed despite beta exclusion.
  16. Prescription crop misses bottom-half stop instructions.
  17. Pharmacist substitution creates accidental new active medicine instead of same-therapy mapping.
  18. Old reminders survive prescription update because stop-date logic is weak.
  19. User changes schedule manually after doctor change but refill logic stays stale.
  20. NRI caregiver receives noisy routine alerts and ignores the one important exception.
  21. Caregiver burden increases because app reminds caregiver to chase patient instead of first asking patient.
  22. Copy uses alarmist certainty: “missed/danger/failure” when system only knows “unmarked.”
  23. No audit trail for who changed a medicine and when.
  24. Prescription verification UI hides confidence and source image crop.
  25. Beta onboarding accepts complex households outside scope, overwhelming support and safety checks.

Top 25 highest-retention moments

  1. First verified prescription becomes a clean daily schedule.
  2. Caregiver sees exact timestamp after parent marks dose done.
  3. App catches a duplicate brand/generic before activation.
  4. Refill alert arrives 5–7 days before stock gap.
  5. Doctor visit prompt makes updating prescription easy.
  6. Weekly summary says stock and doses are mostly on track.
  7. Caregiver can distinguish unmarked vs missed vs stock risk.
  8. Old medicine is clearly stopped after prescription update.
  9. User sees side-by-side old vs new prescription changes.
  10. OCR uncertainty is handled honestly instead of guessed.
  11. Local-language or family-native copy feels respectful.
  12. NRI caregiver receives a calm exception-only digest.
  13. Patient gets large Taken / Not yet actions.
  14. Snooze pattern triggers timing adjustment suggestion.
  15. App recognizes pharmacy substitution as same medicine.
  16. Refill list is ready before medical-store trip.
  17. Caregiver shares doctor-ready medicine history.
  18. System prevents unsupported insulin/injection entry during beta.
  19. Prescription age warning prevents blind continuation.
  20. Family member sees “nothing urgent today” reassurance.
  21. Caregiver receives praise for continuity without fake gamification.
  22. Missed-dose recovery says follow doctor advice, not self-correct.
  23. App asks if a daily reminder time is bad after repeated snoozes.
  24. Medicine strip count update is quick and practical.
  25. Onboarding narrows scope honestly and sets expectations.

Top 10 silent churn signals

  1. Delivered reminders but no opens for 7+ days.
  2. Dose confirmations happen within 1–2 seconds repeatedly.
  3. Repeated snooze at same time without schedule edit.
  4. Caregiver stops opening weekly summaries.
  5. Prescription update prompt ignored after known doctor visit.
  6. Refill alerts dismissed while stock risk rises.
  7. Support messages mention “app is confusing/scary/wrong.”
  8. Caregiver creates parallel WhatsApp/manual tracker.
  9. Notifications disabled after a false alarm.
  10. Old prescription remains active beyond staleness threshold.

Top 10 trust-preserving UX principles

  1. Say “unmarked” unless actual non-adherence is verified.
  2. Never activate OCR output without human confirmation.
  3. Show old vs new medicine changes explicitly.
  4. Treat formulation, strength, frequency, timing, and stop-date as separate safety fields.
  5. Preserve uncertainty instead of faking confidence.
  6. Escalate by pattern, not by one missed tap.
  7. Make caregiver reassurance quiet, timestamped, and exception-based.
  8. Use family-native, non-clinical language.
  9. Keep an audit trail for every medication change.
  10. Respect beta scope; reject unsafe complexity early.

Top 10 workflow rules Swasthi must never violate

  1. Never auto-activate a prescription scan.
  2. Never merge or replace medicines silently.
  3. Never allow duplicate molecule/combo conflicts without warning.
  4. Never let old reminders survive a confirmed stop.
  5. Never call an unmarked dose a missed dose by default.
  6. Never hide prescription age/staleness.
  7. Never overwrite specialist prescriptions with GP updates without conflict review.
  8. Never support excluded beta categories accidentally.
  9. Never use alarmist or guilt copy for routine chronic care.
  10. Never remove the caregiver/patient verification step for speed.

Clusters

  • Ideal beta users: English-comfortable adult-child caregivers, one chronic parent, 2–4 stable oral meds, high refill pain.
  • Risky beta users: recent hospitalization, frequent doctor conflicts, alternate-day schedules, low app literacy.
  • Likely churners: low trust in apps, already using manual family WhatsApp effectively, no refill pain.
  • High-referral users: NRI caregivers, tech-comfortable families, users who avoid a real refill gap.
  • High-support-burden users: multi-patient homes, local-language-only users, complex regimens, excluded medicine categories.

Product verdict

Swasthi should not position itself as “AI medication management.” That invites overtrust. The safer, stronger positioning is: Your family’s medication clarity system. The product wins if it is boringly reliable: fewer guesses, clearer verification, cleaner updates, calmer reminders, and refill continuity that actually prevents gaps.

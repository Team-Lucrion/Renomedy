What does Swasthi at 5 years feel like?
It does not feel like an app. It feels like background infrastructure — invisible until needed, like a smoke detector. The caregiver rarely opens it, but it opens itself at the right moment: a refill warning three days before the medicine runs out, a compliance summary before the next doctor's visit, a gentle prompt when the prescription must be renewed. The elderly parent has stopped calling it "that phone reminder" — they call it "my medicine alarm." The NRI child in London checks it on Sunday mornings between chai and the newspaper, not anxiously but habitually, the way you check the front door is locked. The product is no longer experienced as a product. That is the only version of Swasthi that survives for 5 years.
